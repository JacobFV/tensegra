## Minimal reproducible pricing recommendation

Freeze a **public neural-work tariff** before training/main selection. Calibrate architecture/forward/microstep units using warmed representative batch-one profiles, or explicitly use deterministic estimated arithmetic-work units. Charge every actual scoring invocation and microstep, including rejected calls and failed episodes. Keep the tariff table, profiling batch shapes, synchronization policy and measured variation in the frozen protocol.

Population fitness may use this deterministic price alongside observation/action/travel and solver expansion prices. Label it modeled priced work, not per-episode measured CPU or GPU consumption. Actual inclusive CPU, reserved GPU occupancy, latency and quality/cost Pareto curves remain independently reported and budgeted. Solver work units similarly are algorithm-specific and are not seconds.

Do not use noisy co-batched measured latency directly as the training/selection target: then neighboring episodes, batching order or competing host load can alter a policy's reward without changing its decisions. Charge by fixed public rules and test price shifts; audit real runtime separately. A hardware-calibrated tariff is a benchmark contract, not a universal economic cost.

A lightweight controller should retain its lower tariff when justified by measured work. Heuristics also incur computation: record algorithm work and actual runtime, rather than calling them free. Comparisons can show both task utility under the frozen public prices and actual measured total cost. Never add per-call CPU diagnostics to the already inclusive outer experiment receipt.

No new architecture or broad sweep is recommended by this review. The first informative experiment remains whether learned control acquires a cost crossover against capable cheap-first and same-observation tool references.
