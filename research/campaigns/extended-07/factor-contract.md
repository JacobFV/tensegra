## Summary

1. **There are exactly 23 coordinates.** `N_FACTOR_FEATURES = 23`, checked against the code. The code comments group them into 7 blocks, which this contract maps onto the brief's 4 thematic groups:

   | group | coordinates |
   |---|---|
   | G1 posterior/outcome probabilities | 0–7 |
   | G2 side/event costs | 8–10 |
   | G3 one-query strategy-cost estimates | 11–15 |
   | G4 amortized build + bookkeeping | 16–22 |

   The extended-06 report describes LRN's heads as predicting "four derived quantities". That understates the target: it is one 23-dimensional regression.
2. **Every coordinate is a function of the public configuration and the public information state.** Nothing reads hidden state. Three mechanical checks confirm this:
   - an independent re-implementation from the actor's own public config vector plus the visible history matches `factor_features` at every sampled decision (error < 1e-6);
   - decisions that share a visible history but have different hidden draws get bit-identical factors;
   - the LRN targets logged by the trainer equal the public-history recomputation.

   **No leak was found.**
3. **The G3/G4 strategy-cost coordinates are not Q\*.** They are one-query closed forms of fixed, hand-written continuations. Measured against exact Q\*, their mean absolute error is 3–27 price units, against a decision tolerance ε of 0.5 (numbers in the Q\* comparison section below).
4. **Semantic defects and inconsistencies** (details in the numerical-consistency section):
   - (a) `candidate_trust` applies the probe likelihood twice under U, so it is overconfident: it exceeds bH on 39% of probe-candidate decisions (mean +.040, max +.37).
   - (b) `cost_probe_first_rel` omits the forgone reward R of a false "solved".
   - (c) `cost_b1_first_rel` and `cost_use_rel` omit the event re-run that `cost_exact_b2_rel` and `cost_probe_first_rel` include.
   - (d) `p_probe_resolves` is identical to `belief_H`, so it gets double weight in the MSE.
   - (e) `exp_hard_cost_rel` is 0 and `steps_left_rel` is 1 on every b6/b6c configuration, because the T and D factors never occur there.
   - (f) The ±5 clip binds on `cost_use_rel` (0.45% of decisions) and `build_value_rel` (1.1%).
   - (g) `build_value_rel` alone carries 32% of the target variance and `cost_use_rel` 11%, so the equal-weight aux MSE is dominated by amortization coordinates.

## The 23 coordinates

**Notation:**
- b = (bH, bM, bF, bX) is the posterior over the hidden type.
- rem = max(k − i, 1).
- hazard = p_event if no event has happened yet in this query, else 0.
- hard = t_hard · c_b2 · (bF + bX).
- side = D_side · (1 − bH).
- b2route = (c_b2 + hard)(1 + hazard).
- Prices are divided by R = 100 (a generator constant; R itself is not an input).
- "b6 range" is the observed [min, max] over 176,724 uniform-random-policy decisions on b6-like families: the training types plus S+C+E and U+C+E, from 20,000 dev_smoke configurations.

**Column codes:**
- **Kind:**
  - **EX**: exact function of the public state (a Bayes posterior or a one-step outcome probability).
  - **BK**: bookkeeping readout.
  - **AM**: approximate, myopic, one-query closed form of a fixed continuation. It is not Q\*, not π\*, and not the agent's policy.
- **Use:**
  - **SUP**: an exact input to the fusion layer in the SUP arm. It is not fed to the GRU.
  - **LRN**: a regression target only. The policy reads the stop-gradient prediction.
  - RAWF uses no coordinate (φ = 0).

  Every coordinate is SUP / LRN.

No coordinate is policy-dependent as a function. The **state distribution** on which LRN is trained is the learner's own on-policy distribution, which moves during training.

### Grouping and justification

**G1 (0–7):** the posterior and the one-step outcome probabilities of the two cheap computations (probe, b1). They are exact.

**G2 (8–10):** the per-factor costs, in expectation under the belief: S side charge, T surcharge, E hazard. They are exact expectations of single charges.

**G3 (11–15):** the closed-form strategy costs, which are myopic. Coordinate 14 includes the build amortization. It stays in G3 because it is the fourth strategy, which the argmin (f15) ranges over.

**G4 (16–22):** the amortized build value plus the bookkeeping readouts.

`candidate_trust` is probability-like, but it sits in G4 because it is a readout of the candidate slot, gated by f19/f20. The code's own 7 comment blocks are kept in the JSON as `code_block`.

**Core state.** Given the public configuration, all 23 coordinates are a deterministic function of a small public core:
- belief (3 degrees of freedom);
- `reduced`;
- `ev` (an event has happened this query);
- query index i;
- `built`;
- `cand_ok`;
- `cand_exact`;
- deadline steps.

A **dependency-consistent group replacement** in Phase 1 should edit the core and recompute `factor_features`. Editing a coordinate in isolation breaks the identities below. `replacement_closure` in the JSON lists, for every coordinate, all coordinates that are functions of it; for example, belief_H → {4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 13, 15, 16, 21}.

## Public-history reconstructibility (no leak)

**Actor information.** The actor's inputs are:
- the public config vector (`Config6.public_vector`, 31 entries: prices / R, flags, q, corr, p_event, η, p_conflict, k, ρ, the prior, deadline, t_hard);
- the previous visible record (action, outcome, event, reveal);
- the available-action mask;
- the query fraction i/k.

The GRU carries the rest of the history.

**Everything `factor_features` reads is recoverable from these inputs:**
- R-scaled prices, q, D_side, L and p_event are read directly.
- c_use = C_execute + C_return + C_verify.
- C_build is clipped at 10R in the vector's entry 6. It is still recoverable exactly from the ρ entry, because ρ ≥ .09 > the 1e-3 floor in every generator cell (tested).
- The belief needs only the prior, q, η, p_conflict, corr, the outcomes and the terminal reveals. The verifier's reveal after commit or abstain is public by design.

**Mechanical tests** (`tests/test_campaign07_factor_contract.py`; all pass locally and on pro6000):

## Numerical consistency

The audit covers 20,000 dev_smoke configurations and 228,762 decisions under a uniform-random policy, split into two scopes: b6-like (176,724) and with D or T (52,038). Results are in [p0-audit/factor_audit.json](p0-audit/factor_audit.json).

**Clean results:**
- **Probabilities.** f0–7, f10 and f21 always lie in [0, 1]. f0 + … + f3 = 1 to 1.0e-12. There are no non-finite values.
- **Units.** Every cost is price / R. The decision tolerance ε = 0.5 price units is **.005** in these units.
- **Missing-value conventions.** Several coordinates use 0 as a sentinel:
  - f6 = 0 means "undefined";
  - f16 = 0 means "built";
  - f21 = 0 means "no valid candidate";
  - f22 = 1 means "no deadline".

  f6 is the only sentinel with no disambiguating coordinate, though bH = 0 identifies it.

**Dependency identities** (maximum residual over all decisions; used by the Phase-1 replacement code):

| identity | max residual |
|---|---|
| f4 = f0 | 0 |
| f5 = (1 − f0)(1 − q) | 0 |
| f6 = f0 / (f0 + f5) (0 if 0/0) | 0 |
| f7 = f0 + f1 + f2·reduced | 0 |
| f8 = (D_side / R)(1 − f0) | 1.1e-16 |
| f15 = min(f11..f14) | 0 |
| f20 ≤ f19 | 0 |
| f21 = f20 + (f19 − f20)·f6 | 0 |

f9, f11–f14 and f16 are functions of f0–f3, f7, f10 and the public config (see `depends_on` in the JSON).

**Clipping at ±5** (coordinates 11–16), as the fraction of decisions whose unclipped value exceeds 5:

| scope | f14 cost_use | f16 build_value | f13 cost_b1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| b6-like | .0045 | .0110 (both tails) | 0 |
| with D/T | .0051 | .0213 | .0021 |

The clipped values are recoverable from the public config (C_build, rem, c_use), so the clip loses no *state* information. It does saturate the target: the auxiliary predictor is never asked to represent build values above 5R. f9 is unclipped (≤ 3.1).

**Target scale.** The aux loss is an unweighted MSE over the 23 coordinates. Shares of the total target variance (b6-like, random policy):

| coordinates | share |
|---|---|
| build_value_rel | .318 |
| cost_use_rel | .112 |
| built | .069 |
| candidate_valid / candidate_exact / candidate_trust | .066 / .061 / .064 |
| p_H_given_solved | .044 |
| each belief coordinate | .011–.034 |
| exp_side_cost_rel | .002 |
| event_hazard_active | .012 |
| f9, f22 | 0 |

Two consequences:
- The equal-weight MSE is dominated by amortization and bookkeeping coordinates. The belief and hazard coordinates, which carry the C/E interaction, are cheap to get wrong.
- The historical training-distribution aux MSE (≈ .0055–.0064; ext-06 audit) is an RMS of ≈ .075 per coordinate on average. That is 15× ε in the cost coordinates' units.

Phase 1 should therefore report error per coordinate and per group, normalized per coordinate, and never the pooled MSE alone.

**Defects:**

1. **`candidate_trust` (f21) double-counts under U.** After a probe reports "solved", `advance` has already conditioned the belief on it, so bH = P(H | history) = P(commit of the probe candidate is correct). f21 = bH / (bH + (1 − bH)(1 − q)) conditions on "solved" a second time.
   - On 10,866 probe-candidate decisions, f21 − bH averages +.040 (max +.368) and is > 0 on 39%.
   - With q = 1 the two coincide, so S+C+E (no U) is unaffected.
   - This matters for LRN's training targets on U-containing training types.
   - A corrected coordinate (= bH on probe candidates) must get a new name. The test pins the historical definition.
2. **Strategy-cost bookkeeping is inconsistent across strategies.**
   - Probe-first omits the forgone reward of a wrong commit. Its cost is not utility-consistent unless you add f5·R.
   - The event re-run is included for b2 and probe but omitted for b1 and use.
   - b2 re-runs even on X.
3. **Availability is not encoded.** f4–f7 and f11–f16 are reported even when the strategy's first action is unavailable: the probe was already used this epoch, b1 after b1 or b2, or the deadline is exhausted. At such states these coordinates are hypothetical.
4. **Redundancy.** f4 ≡ f0. f9 ≡ 0 and f22 ≡ 1 on all b6/b6c data. f15 is a function of f11–f14. Given the config, only 11 of the 23 coordinates are functionally independent: f0–f3, f7, f10, f17–f20 and f22, which is 10 degrees of freedom since the belief sums to 1. On b6/b6c data, f22 is constant as well.

## The closed-form strategy costs are not Q\*

The comparison uses 200 dev_smoke configurations: training types plus S+C+E, with k = 1 (100) and k = 2 (100). Each is solved by exact DP and evaluated at every decision of a π\* trajectory and a random trajectory. Results are in [p0-audit/factor_qstar.json](p0-audit/factor_qstar.json).

For k = 1, utility = R·success − costs, so **R − Q\*(s, a)** is the exact expected cost, including forgone reward, of "take the strategy's first action, then act optimally". It is compared with the closed form f·R.

| k = 1 (price units; ε = 0.5) | n | mean (closed − exact) | mean \|diff\| | frac \|diff\| > ε | frac closed < exact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cost_exact_b2 | 553 | +4.10 | 4.10 | .22 | 0 |
| cost_probe_first | 479 | +26.99 | 27.14 | .98 | .021 |
| cost_probe_first + f5·R (utility-consistent) | 479 | +35.02 | 35.02 | .98 | 0 |
| cost_b1_first | 464 | +7.93 | 8.79 | .72 | .095 |
| cost_use (build / use) | 584 | +2.47 | 3.00 | .48 | .147 |
| best_strategy_cost vs R − V\* | 598 | +18.58 | 18.90 | .87 | .028 |

**Reading:**
- Where the closed form is the cost of a *feasible* fixed continuation (b2; probe + forgone R), it upper-bounds the exact value, as expected: 0% below. π\* then does better by gathering information (inspect, prop), falling back to b1, or abstaining.
- The rows that fall below exact (b1, use, raw probe) are exactly those that omit an event re-run or the forgone reward.
- **Decision content.** The closed-form argmin strategy's first action is ε-optimal at only 36% (k = 1) and 38% (k = 2) of decisions. Restricted to decisions where some strategy's first action is ε-optimal, agreement is .81 (k = 1) and .74 (k = 2).
- For k ≥ 2 the closed forms ignore cross-query value, such as the information carried by reveals under C. That is exactly where extended-06's LRN lost flip accuracy (query 2 after H).

Conclusion: the G3/G4 targets are myopic heuristic features. They are neither Q\* nor a policy's value.
